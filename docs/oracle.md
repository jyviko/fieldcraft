## Sources and tolerances

- **[bar-pressure-wave](../tests/oracle/bar-pressure-wave/)** (theory): Elastic wave in a bar from a pressure step (explicit). Source: d'Alembert's solution of the one-dimensional wave equation: c = √(E/ρ), particle velocity v = p/(ρc); Graff, Wave Motion in Elastic Solids, Dover 1991, §2.1. Tolerances — u_end: the end moves at the particle velocity p/(ρc) from the start; the discrete wave's ringing behind the front changes the end's displacement by well under 1%; v_mid: central differences ring behind a step front (dispersion); bulk viscosity damps it within a few elements, leaving about 1% twenty elements behind; balance: external work = kinetic + internal + dissipated energy; the solver warns above 1%
- **[bar-rigid-wall-impact](../tests/oracle/bar-rigid-wall-impact/)** (theory): Elastic bar striking a rigid wall (explicit). Source: One-dimensional elastic impact on a rigid wall: contact time 2L/c, rebound at the impact speed, contact stress ρcv₀; Graff, Wave Motion in Elastic Solids, Dover 1991, §2.3; Johnson, Impact Strength of Materials, Arnold 1972, §1.6. Tolerances — u_front: the contact ends at 2L/c in the elastic theory; the discrete release front is spread over a few elements, a few tenths of a percent of the time, and the rebound speed is v₀ to about 1%; u_back: as for the struck end; ke: an elastic impact keeps the kinetic energy; quadratic bulk viscosity in the compression front and the vibration left in the bar take about 1%
- **[cantilever-frequencies](../tests/oracle/cantilever-frequencies/)** (theory): Natural frequencies of a cantilever (Euler–Bernoulli). Source: Euler–Bernoulli beam: f_n = (β_n L)²/(2π L²)·√(EI/ρA), β₁L = 1.8751, β₂L = 4.6941; Timoshenko, Young and Weaver, Vibration Problems in Engineering, 4th ed., Wiley 1974, §5.3. Tolerances — f1: Euler–Bernoulli neglects shear and rotary inertia, which lower f1 by about 0.2% at L/h = 20; f1_pair: as f1: a square section bends alike in y and z; f2: shear and rotary inertia lower the second mode by about 1% at L/h = 20 (Timoshenko)
- **[cantilever-hex20](../tests/oracle/cantilever-hex20/)** (theory): Cantilever under a tip load, Timoshenko deflection (hex20). Source: Timoshenko and Gere, Mechanics of Materials (beam with shear deformation: δ = PL³/3EI + PL/κGA, κ = 5/6 for a rectangle); Cowper, J. Appl. Mech. 33 (1966) 335–340 for the shear coefficient. Tolerances — tip_uz: beam theory vs 3D elasticity: the shear coefficient and the fully clamped end change the tip deflection by a few tenths of a percent at L/h = 10; reaction: equilibrium holds to rounding
- **[cantilever-hex8-incompatible](../tests/oracle/cantilever-hex8-incompatible/)** (theory): Cantilever under a tip load, Timoshenko deflection (hex8-incompatible). Source: Timoshenko and Gere, Mechanics of Materials (beam with shear deformation: δ = PL³/3EI + PL/κGA, κ = 5/6 for a rectangle); Cowper, J. Appl. Mech. 33 (1966) 335–340 for the shear coefficient. Tolerances — tip_uz: beam theory vs 3D elasticity (a few tenths of a percent at L/h = 10); incompatible modes represent the bending exactly in the constant-moment part; reaction: equilibrium holds to rounding
- **[cantilever-tet10](../tests/oracle/cantilever-tet10/)** (theory): Cantilever under a tip load, Timoshenko deflection (tet10). Source: Timoshenko and Gere, Mechanics of Materials (beam with shear deformation: δ = PL³/3EI + PL/κGA, κ = 5/6 for a rectangle); Cowper, J. Appl. Mech. 33 (1966) 335–340 for the shear coefficient. Tolerances — tip_uz: beam theory vs 3D elasticity (a few tenths of a percent at L/h = 10); quadratic tetrahedra are converged on this mesh; reaction: equilibrium holds to rounding
- **[cdt-cylinder](../tests/oracle/cdt-cylinder/)** (theory): Constrained Delaunay mesh of a cylinder: volume within the chordal bound, valid elements. Source: mesh invariants: an inscribed polygon of sagitta s loses at most 2s/R of a circle's area (elementary geometry); constrained Delaunay tetrahedralization after Diazzi et al., ACM TOG 42(6) 2023. Tolerances — volume: the tessellation inscribes the cylinder within the chordal tolerance s = 0.05: the polygon section loses between 0 and 2s/R = 0.5% of the area; the reference is the middle of that band; invalid: every element of a valid mesh has positive volume
- **[cdt-stepped-block](../tests/oracle/cdt-stepped-block/)** (theory): Constrained Delaunay mesh of a stepped block: exact volume, valid elements. Source: mesh invariants: a tetrahedralization of a polyhedron fills its volume exactly (Si, TetGen paper ACM TOMS 41(2) 2015 for the definition of a CDT; Diazzi et al., ACM TOG 42(6) 2023); the volume is 60·40·10 + 40·40·10 + 20·40·10 − 10·10·10. Tolerances — volume: a planar boundary is tessellated exactly and the constrained Delaunay mesh fills it exactly: only rounding; invalid: every element of a valid mesh has positive volume; undetermined: a linear tetrahedron's sign is exact
- **[free-beam-frequencies](../tests/oracle/free-beam-frequencies/)** (theory): Natural frequencies of a free beam: rigid-body modes and the first bending mode. Source: Euler–Bernoulli free–free beam, β₁L = 4.7300; Timoshenko, Young and Weaver, Vibration Problems in Engineering, 4th ed., Wiley 1974, §5.3. Tolerances — rigid_6: a free body has six zero frequencies; numerical rigid-body frequencies below 10⁻³ of the first elastic one (eigenvalues below 10⁻⁶ of it) are rounding of the shifted solve; f7: Euler–Bernoulli neglects shear and rotary inertia, about 0.8% at L/h = 20 for the free–free mode; mass: the mass matrix integrates the density exactly
- **[j2-bauschinger](../tests/oracle/j2-bauschinger/)** (theory): Bauschinger effect of linear kinematic hardening: tension, then back to zero strain. Source: J2 plasticity with Prager's linear kinematic hardening in uniaxial stress (W. Prager, Ing.-Arch. 1955; Simo and Hughes, Computational Inelasticity, Springer 1998, §2.3): from σ_a = σ_y + E_t (ε_a − σ_y/E) the material unloads elastically by 2σ_y and then hardens with E_t, so at zero strain σ = σ_a − 2σ_y − E_t (ε_a − 2σ_y/E). Tolerances — residual_stress: a homogeneous small-strain state the element represents exactly, with increments that end at the load path's turn; isotropic hardening would give −281.835 (it yields again only at −σ_a); plastic_strain: (σ_a − σ_y)/H forward plus E/(E + H) (ε_a − 2σ_y/E) in reverse
- **[j2-heated-bar](../tests/oracle/j2-heated-bar/)** (theory): A steel bar held at its ends and heated past yield (small strain). Source: Uniaxial J2 plasticity with linear hardening (Simo and Hughes, Computational Inelasticity, Springer 1998, §2.3) with the additive thermal strain: the bar's total axial strain is held at zero, so its mechanical strain is −α ΔT and σ = −(σ_y + E_t (α ΔT − σ_y/E)) once α ΔT exceeds σ_y/E. Tolerances — sigma: a homogeneous uniaxial state the elements represent exactly; plastic_strain: (\|σ\| − σ_y)/H, as for the stress
- **[j2-uniaxial-explicit](../tests/oracle/j2-uniaxial-explicit/)** (theory): Uniaxial tension of a J2 bilinear-hardening cube to large strain (explicit). Source: von Mises plasticity with linear isotropic hardening under uniaxial stress (Simo and Hughes, Computational Inelasticity, Springer 1998, §2.3); logarithmic strain for a hypoelastic–plastic law under coaxial stretching. Tolerances — sigma: uniaxial J2 with linear hardening: σ = σ_y + E_t (ε − σ_y/E) in logarithmic strain; the stretch takes 10⁴ wave transits, so inertia and the ringing of the start (ρcv ≈ 0.1% of σ) stay well under 1%; plastic_strain: ε_p = ε − σ/E, as for the stress
