# Roadmap

FieldCraft's goal is CAE modeling on par with Abaqus/CAE, Nastran pre-processors and LS-PrePost,
with hexahedral meshing as the centre of gravity, plus its own linear solver. Abaqus/CAE is the
reference for scope (its documentation is excellent and its solver strong); its application is
where we aim to be clearly better: parametric geometry from SolidCraft, sets that survive
remeshing, automatic hex partitioning, undo, a text-diffable model and agent control.
The capability checklist is [docs/parity.md](docs/parity.md). Beyond the pre-processor and the
linear solver come our own explicit, CFD (lattice Boltzmann) and fluid–structure solvers (M10–M14;
methods and papers in [docs/solvers/literature.md](docs/solvers/literature.md)).

Order (revised 2026-10-13): one end-to-end slice first — *SolidCraft part → named faces → sets →
mesh → material and section → BCs and loads → static and frequency steps → Abaqus, Nastran and
LS-DYNA decks and our own solve → contour plot* — on tet meshes, so every hex mesher lands in a
pipeline that already produces solvable, verified models. Then hex: structured (sweep and mapped,
with partitions proposed automatically) before octree all-hex, because that is how analysts get
hex meshes today and it gives the best elements where stresses are read.

Revised 2026-10-14 after a gap review (below): the implicit side is linear today, and nearly
everything analysts ask for next — contact and friction, elastomers, viscoelasticity, creep,
adhesion, composite failure, tissues — needs a nonlinear implicit solver first. The nonlinear
core (M15) therefore comes right after automatic hex (M6) and before contact (M7). Every material
model is written once in `fieldcraft-fem` with its consistent tangent and serves the implicit and
explicit solvers alike; every coupled field (temperature, pressure, electric potential, pore
pressure) is a degree of freedom of the existing DOF framework, not a separate solver.

## Next

In progress, in this order of landing:

1. Tet quality: sliver repair and Delaunay refinement to a size field, so curved and filleted parts
   (the filleted bracket) solve on tets (M2).
2. Octree all-hex for any part (M6), the project's main meshing goal.
3. The analysis video regenerated with the setup's symbols (done: support, load, reference-point
   and coupling symbols in the viewport and headless renders), captions and an orbit (screenshot
   camera and the ribbon following the command: done).
4. A Scordelis–Lo refinement study (32×32×2, 64×64×4, hex20 full and reduced) separating
   discretisation error from the solid-versus-shell model difference (−0.33 % today).
5. The nonlinear implicit core (M15), the gate for contact (M7), elastomers and viscoelasticity
   (M17), composite failure (M18), adhesion (M20) and tissues (M21): solids, hyperelasticity and
   follower pressure done; J2 plasticity and Mooney–Rivlin on the material point interface next.
